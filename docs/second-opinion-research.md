# Smart Surgical Second Opinion Network — prior-art research

Researched 2026-11-22. Web search only (English-language). **Not searched:** Arabic-
and Kurdish-language sources, Iraqi/KRG government gazettes, Kurdistan Medical
Syndicate bylaws. Prices come from providers' own pages and change often.

**How claims were checked.** Rows marked ✔ were re-read on the provider's own page
on 2026-11-22. Rows marked ~ come from search summaries of the provider's page
and were not re-opened.

## Short answer

Yes, many have done it — but almost all of it is either (a) big US academic
centres selling a written report, (b) provider-to-provider networks where a
local doctor asks a central expert, (c) employer/insurer benefits, or (d)
Turkish/Gulf hospitals offering free opinions as a funnel for medical travel.

**I found no surgeon-led second-opinion service based in Kurdistan or Iraq.**
The closest competitors in your patients' path are Acıbadem (free opinion in
~24 h, offices in **Erbil and Duhok**) and Indian/Turkish medical-travel brokers.

## The models that exist

### 1. Academic centre, written report, self-pay

| Provider | How it works | Price | Turnaround | Referral |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ✔ Cleveland Clinic (MyConsult / Virtual Second Opinions) | Register → nurse video intake (US) → they collect records → matched to a specialist → written report, optional video visit. Two tiers: *Guided Review* (report) and *Physician Video Review* (report + visit) | US $1,690 report / $1,990 report + visit; **outside US $4,500**; not usually insured | Match "in a few days" after records; ~2 weeks overall | Not required |
| ✔ UCLA (neurosurgery remote opinion) | Teleconsult, video or written; established diagnosis only; out-of-state only | $1,000 | — | **Written referral from treating doctor required** |
| ~ Emory (neurosurgery) | Fully remote written report | $500 | 7–10 business days | — |
| ~ Atrium Health | Remote record review | $500 | 7 business days after records + payment | — |
| ~ Penn Medicine | Virtual second opinion, self-pay | from $2,000 | — | — |
| ~ Johns Hopkins | Dedicated **Thyroid Second Opinion Service** for complex or hard-to-manage thyroid disease | — | — | — |
| ~ Dana-Farber | Online second opinion worldwide; re-reads original biopsies | — | — | — |

Common pattern: a named specialist signs a **written report**; the service
explicitly is *not* the treating doctor; emergencies excluded; records collected
centrally; nurse or coordinator triages before a surgeon sees it.

### 2. Provider-to-provider network (closest to the word "network")

- ~ **Mayo Clinic Care Network** — 50+ independent member organisations, ~80
  hospitals. Not a merger: members stay independent and pay to join. Tools:
  **eConsults** (member doctor asks a Mayo specialist about a patient; one member
  reports answers in under a week; some members don't charge the patient) and
  **eTumor Boards** (virtual multidisciplinary case conferences). The patient's
  own doctor initiates, not the patient.
- ~ **Cleveland Clinic affiliate/alliance hospitals** — second-opinion service
  for partner hospitals since 2015.
- ~ **Virtual tumour boards in low/middle-income countries** — India's National
  Cancer Grid runs head & neck VTBs (most decisions judged implementable; valued
  for teaching); the Global Neuroblastoma Network ran weekly VTBs 2010–2020 with
  575 participants from 77 countries. Evidence is feasibility and participation,
  not patient outcomes.

### 3. Employer / insurer benefit

- ~ **Teladoc Expert Medical Opinion** (formerly Best Doctors, bought 2017 for
  $440 M): free to employees, paid by the employer; claims 50,000+ experts.
  ProPublica (2019) criticised Best Doctors' pay-to-play physician awards.
- ~ **2nd.MD** — partnered with UnitedHealthcare.
- ~ Cleveland Clinic MyConsult is also bundled by insurers (e.g. Discovery Health,
  South Africa).

### 4. Statutory right (Germany)

~ Under **§27b SGB V** and the G-BA *Zweitmeinungs-Richtlinie*, patients have a
legal right to an independent second opinion before listed elective operations
(e.g. tonsil surgery, hysterectomy, knee replacement, spinal surgery, diabetic-foot
amputation, carotid revascularisation; the list keeps growing). The surgeon must
tell the patient at least 10 days before. Second-opinion doctors must meet
qualification rules and must not perform the operation themselves.
**Thyroid surgery was not on any list I saw** — not confirmed against the full
current directive.

### 5. Regional hospitals — free opinion as a funnel

| Provider | Model |
|---|---|
| ✔ **Acıbadem** (Turkey) | **Free**, no referral, no obligation. Upload reports/scans (8 MB/file) or send by WhatsApp. "Most opinions within 24 hours"; multidisciplinary board for complex cases. **Offices in Erbil and Duhok.** |
| ~ Memorial (Turkey) | Online second opinion by video call |

### 6. Commercial aggregators (unverified quality)

~ Medigence (report review from $75, full record from $125, 3–5 days),
EnrichCarePlus (has an Iraq page), MyUSADr, Medebound, Healthverse (UAE),
Doctoury (Iraqi booking app that pitches online consults for second opinions).

## Iraq / Kurdistan context

- ~ **Medical travel:** 30,989 Iraqis travelled to India for treatment in 2025
  (India Ministry of Tourism, via Shafaq News, July 2026) — second only to
  Bangladesh. Patients complained of inflated bills and intermediaries; the report
  itself advises getting an **independent opinion before travelling**. That is an
  open position SST could hold.
- ~ **Law:** no specific telemedicine law found for Iraq; a legal analysis says the
  1925 Law on the Practice of Medicine applies by default and does not define
  "consultation". A 2025 review of Kurdistan digital health says the region lacks a
  regulatory framework. **Not checked:** KRG Ministry of Health instructions and
  the Kurdistan Medical Syndicate's rules — these need a direct question.

## What the evidence says a second opinion is worth (for our conditions)

- ~ Mayo (Van Such 2017, *J Eval Clin Pract*, 286 referrals): 12% same diagnosis,
  66% refined, **21% distinctly different**. The often-quoted "88%" adds refined +
  different; single centre, primary-care referrals.
- ~ **Thyroid FNA cytology re-review** (Korea, *Endocr J* 2012, 1,499 patients):
  26.3% disagreement; management changed in 13.7% of the disagreements.
  Stanford: second review of *indeterminate* FNAs raised accuracy 60% → 74% and
  avoided a diagnostic operation in 25% (32/129). Turkey (101 patients): 76%
  concordance, management changed in 21. All retrospective, single-centre.
- ~ Roswell Park reports diagnosis changes in ~11–18% of secondary reviews.
- **Limit:** neck ultrasound is operator-dependent. UCLA prefers in-person first
  visits because its surgeons scan the neck themselves; MSK re-images the whole
  neck. A report-only review of someone else's ultrasound report is weak.

## Things that need your decision before anything is built

1. **"Network" or "service"?** Every network found (Mayo, Cleveland alliance,
   tumour-board groups) links *several institutions or outside experts*. If only
   SST surgeons answer, "Network" overstates it — call it the *Smart Surgical
   Second Opinion Service* until outside surgeons actually join.
2. **It is not `/ask`.** `/ask` was decided as *general information only, no
   uploads, personal cases → book a visit, deleted after 6 months*. A second
   opinion is the opposite: a personal case, uploaded records, a signed report.
   That means identity checks, explicit consent, private file storage, and a
   **medical-record retention period** far longer than 6 months. It needs its own
   flow, table and storage — reusing `/ask` would break what was promised there.
3. **What gets reviewed.** The thyroid evidence says the value is in re-reading
   **slides and images**, not reports. Decide whether you accept only reports
   (cheap, weak), images/DICOM, or physical slides sent to a named pathologist.
   The report should be allowed to say "needs re-imaging / slide review in person".
4. **Free or paid.** Acıbadem is free and in Erbil/Duhok, but free there is a
   funnel to surgery in Turkey. A paid, independent opinion ("we will not operate
   unless you choose us") is a different and arguably more credible promise —
   compare Germany's rule that the second-opinion doctor doesn't operate.
5. **Scope.** Thyroid, parathyroid and breast only (matches the site), or wider?
6. **Who signs.** A named consultant per report, and whether a pathologist and
   radiologist are part of it (a mini tumour board) for cancer cases.
7. **Legal/ethics.** Ask the KRG Ministry of Health and the Kurdistan Medical
   Syndicate whether written remote opinions are allowed, and confirm Smart Health
   Tower's policy (already open item 5 in `patient-questions-plan.md`).
8. **Architecture (per AGENTS.md this needs your explicit yes).** New entity
   ("network members" / reviewers), a cases table, private uploads in R2, a
   reviewer Admin screen, and a public landing page. Bigger than Patient questions.
